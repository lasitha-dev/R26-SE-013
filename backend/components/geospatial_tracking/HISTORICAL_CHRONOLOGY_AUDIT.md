# Historical Chronology Audit — Checkpoint 4 Part 5

Computed over `historical_chronology.csv`, `target_quality_report_v2.csv`,
and `historical_forecast_targets.csv` (all generated by
`services/build_historical_replay.py` from the real
`canonical_outbreaks_conservative.csv`), restricted throughout to
**dedup-resolved model candidates only** (`model_candidate = True` and
`dedup_status` in `SINGLETON`/`AUTO_MERGED_HIGH`/`MANUALLY_ACCEPTED` —
the same gate `get_eligible_sources` enforces).

> **Checkpoint 4.5 terminology correction:** everywhere this document
> originally said "canonical spatial independence" (a boolean, True/False)
> now refers to **coordinate collision status**
> (`services/coordinate_collision.py`) — `UNIQUE_AMONG_RESOLVED` /
> `SHARED_WITH_RESOLVED` / `SHARED_WITH_UNRESOLVED` / `SHARED_WITH_BOTH`.
> Coordinate uniqueness was never proof of epidemiological/statistical
> independence, and Checkpoint 4's boolean collapsed two genuinely
> different situations (sharing a coordinate with another RESOLVED
> outbreak vs. sharing it only with an UNRESOLVED `REVIEW_LOW`/
> `REVIEW_MEDIUM` candidate) into one "non-independent" bucket. The counts
> below use the corrected terminology; see `VALIDATION_PROTOCOL.md` §5 for
> the full real-corpus breakdown and `services/coordinate_collision.py`'s
> module docstring for the rationale.

## 4. Concentrated epidemic waves vs. long gaps

- **Thailand** shows a sharp, concentrated wave: 4 candidates in 2021-03,
  jumping to 37 (2021-04) and peaking at **185 in 2021-05 alone** (43% of
  Thailand's entire candidate count in one month), tapering through
  2021-06/07 (89, 56), then a long tail with gaps (single-digit months,
  a 2022-06 to 2023-09 gap, then 3 records in 2023-10, one final record
  in 2024-03). This is the Event_3644.pdf national-scale event described
  in Checkpoint 1 — one large epidemic wave, not steady-state reporting.
- **Sri Lanka** is entirely a single short wave: 5 of 6 events in
  2020-09, 1 in 2020-10 — 52 days start to finish.
- **Most CSV-only countries** (Malaysia, Russian Federation, Tunisia,
  etc.) show far more spread-out, lower-density patterns across their
  date ranges rather than one dominant wave — consistent with FAO
  EMPRES-i being a broader surveillance feed rather than one intensively
  studied outbreak.

## 5. Coordinate collision status (post-dedup) — corrected in Checkpoint 4.5

**Coordinate uniqueness is a data-quality/co-location indicator, not
proof of epidemiological or statistical independence** — see
`services/coordinate_collision.py`'s module docstring. This section
originally reported a boolean "spatial independence" here; the corrected,
four-way breakdown is:

Corpus-wide (2587 conservative rows, every row had valid coordinates, 0
missing): **UNIQUE_AMONG_RESOLVED 1954, SHARED_WITH_RESOLVED 42,
SHARED_WITH_UNRESOLVED 555, SHARED_WITH_BOTH 36.** (1954+42+555+36 = 2587.
The old boolean's "non-independent 633" = 42+555+36.)

Sri Lanka: 5 of 6 `UNIQUE_AMONG_RESOLVED`; the exception —
`WAHIS_PDF:Event_3473.pdf:002408` (Chavakachcheri, `AUTO_MERGED_HIGH`) —
is `SHARED_WITH_UNRESOLVED` (0 resolved collisions, 1 unresolved), sharing
its coordinate with exactly one other canonical row:
`FAO_EMPRESI_CSV:...:002066`, `dedup_status = REVIEW_LOW`. This is the
Chavakachcheri CSV row with the unresolved 8-day date discrepancy
documented in Checkpoint 2.5 §22-23 — the coordinate-level finding here
independently corroborates that Checkpoint 2.5 finding from a completely
different angle (spatial coincidence, not date/locality matching), while
correctly declining to call the resolved WAHIS outbreak "non-independent"
on the strength of an unresolved, unconfirmed record alone — the
ambiguity is preserved, not force-merged and not silently promoted to a
definitive independence claim in either direction.

Thailand: the majority `UNIQUE_AMONG_RESOLVED`; most of the remainder are
concentrated among the `APPROXIMATE`-GPS records (98 of Thailand's 432
are `APPROXIMATE`) — exactly the population the Checkpoint 2
approximate-coordinate protection rule exists to flag, now confirmed at
the canonical level too. `SHARED_WITH_RESOLVED`/`SHARED_WITH_BOTH` cases
(genuinely two RESOLVED candidates at the same coordinate) are a stronger
signal than the `SHARED_WITH_UNRESOLVED` cases and are worth separate
manual review attention.

**Critical finding, unchanged in substance by the terminology
correction:** Thailand holds essentially 100% of this corpus's Tier-A
direction/speed target depth. Every CSV-only country has **zero** Tier-A
targets under either variant — not because their events are less real,
but because Tier A (both variants) requires `gps_quality == EXACT` (Part
4's documented rule, unchanged by Checkpoint 4.5), and the FAO EMPRES-i
CSV never states GPS precision at all. This is a data-quality fact, not a
tier-definition artifact chosen to favor Thailand — the same rule was
fixed before these counts were inspected (per `DATA_EXPOSURE_AUDIT.md`
§4, tier definitions were not tuned to produce this result).

## 7. Sri Lanka special note (not automatically a train/test partition)

Per master-prompt Part 12, Sri Lanka's 6 defensible episodes are NOT
treated as an automatic train/validation/test role here — see
`SPLIT_PROTOCOL_DRAFT.md` Part 12 for the full discussion, including the
concrete chronology finding that rules out calling any Sri-Lanka-2020
evaluation "prospective": 11 countries have model-candidate data starting
before Sri Lanka's 2020-09-07 event (284 candidates total), but Thailand
— the country with all the Tier-A depth needed for direction/speed
work — starts only in 2021-03, entirely AFTER Sri Lanka's event. Any
development that wants Thailand's data (which any serious model
development would) makes a Sri-Lanka-2020 evaluation retrospective
relative to that development data, not prospective.

## 8. Countries with enough temporal depth vs. sparse/isolated

**Enough depth for within-country chronological analysis** (candidate
count and date spread that support more than a single-point description):
Thailand, Malaysia, Russian Federation, Tunisia, Republic of Korea,
Cambodia, Indonesia (though only 3 unique dates despite 60 records — see
caveat below), Viet Nam, Italy, China, France, Bhutan, Algeria, Libya,
Nepal (roughly the top 15 in §2).

**Caveat:** count alone is not depth — Indonesia has 60 candidates but
only 3 unique event dates (heavy same-day clustering, likely a batch
report), which is a very different chronological shape from Thailand's
139 unique dates across 432 candidates. Any later chronological-depth
scoring must use unique-date count and spread, not raw candidate count
alone.

**Sparse/isolated only:** the 14 countries listed in §3 (1-4 candidates
each).
